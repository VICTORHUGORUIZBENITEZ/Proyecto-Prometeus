DEEP GEOTHERMAL DISSIPATION SYSTEM - EXCHANGE RIBS
Critical Parameter: 12,000 m² at 60-80m depth / 22°C constant
1. THE FOUNDATION
From 15-20 meters depth onwards, the subsoil is no longer affected by surface climate and enters permanent thermal stability.
In the range of 60 to 80 meters depth, in a massive and healthy basalt massif, the rock temperature is 22°C constant, 365 days a year, with an annual variation of less than ±1°C. It is an infinite and free thermal sink.
The system uses that mass as a radiator.
2. THE RIBS CONCEPT - WHY 12,000 m²
It is not a single tunnel. It is a branched radiator, identical to the fins of an electronic heatsink, but on a geological scale.
The principle governing it is Fourier's Law of Heat Conduction:
q = -k • A • (dT/dx)
q = Thermal power to dissipate (e.g., 1 MW from the data center)
k = Thermal conductivity of basalt (1.7 - 2.5 W/m•K)
A = Total exchange surface in contact with rock (12,000 m²)
dT/dx = Temperature difference between tunnel wall and deep rock.
A is the only variable the engineer can control. k is from nature.
If A is small (e.g., 1,000 m²), to dissipate 1 MW you need a huge temperature gradient. The surrounding rock would heat up to 60-70°C and stop absorbing heat. The system saturates.
With A = 12,000 m², the gradient required to dissipate 1 MW is only 1.5°C to 2.5°C. The rock goes from 22°C to 24.5°C maximum. With such low difference, heat flows continuously, slowly and stably towards the infinity of the massif for 30 years without degradation. No thermal saturation.
Basalt is ideal because besides conducting well, it has a massive thermal storage capacity: 2.4 MJ per m³ per degree. It is a thermal battery.
3. WHY IT IS THE MOST DELICATE PART - MANDATORY DISCIPLINE PROTOCOL
This is the irreversible part of the project. A mistake on the surface can be fixed. A mistake at 70 meters in the rock-concrete contact condemns the entire system. It must be executed with absolute discipline.
DISCIPLINE 1: SEPARATION BETWEEN RIBS
Minimum distance between rib axes: 15 meters.
Justification: Each rib generates a heat bulb around it. If ribs are too close, bulbs overlap. Two ribs at 5 meters distance do not sum their area, they compete. The effective area of 12,000 m² is reduced to 4,000 m² and the system collapses thermally in less than two years.
DISCIPLINA 2: PERFECT THERMAL CONTACT
Leaving air voids between lining and rock is prohibited.
Justification: Air has a conductivity of 0.026 W/m•K, it is a perfect insulator. Basalt has 2.0 W/m•K. A 10mm air void equals 1 meter of basalt in thermal resistance. Lining concrete must be high-conductivity (dosed with basalt dust aggregate) and contact grout must be pressure-injected to guarantee 100% rock-concrete contact around the entire perimeter.
DISCIPLINE 3: MASSIF CLEANING
Use of bentonite, insulating drilling muds or oils in the excavation of ribs is prohibited.
Justification: Any mud film leaves an insulating layer on the basalt wall and kills transfer. Excavation must be clean mechanical (roadheader / diamond wire) and wall must be washed with pressurized water before lining.
DISCIPLINE 4: THERMAL MONITORING
Mandatory installation of 3 PT1000 temperature probes per rib, embedded in rock at 0.5m, 1.5m and 3.0m from the wall.
Justification: Without measurement, the system is blind. If a branch starts accumulating heat above +3°C over the base temperature of 22°C, it must be automatically isolated to allow its recovery. The system must be capable of self-regulation.
Conclusion: The first 60 meters are conventional civil works. The 12,000 m² of ribs between 60 and 80 meters are a thermodynamic precision instrument. If discipline of separation, contact, cleaning and monitoring is respected, an infinite radiator of 22°C with 0.0 liters of water consumption is obtained.
